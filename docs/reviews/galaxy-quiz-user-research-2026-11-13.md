# Galaxy project quiz user research

November 13, 2026. Literature review, source audit and research protocol. This review uses external research and the repository’s November 13 browser audit. It includes no interviews, traffic sample or inquiry outcomes from LEVNAT visitors.

## Decision in brief

Keep three ways into the site visible: browse a known service, get help choosing a direction, or contact the studio. The quiz earns its place if an uncertain visitor can find a relevant next step with less effort than browsing alone. Start comparison with one fully labeled HTML answer group per question. Test the 3D spheres as an optional, synchronized way to preview or select the same answers. Preserve the direct service and inquiry routes in every prototype. The evidence supports testing this arrangement and leaves the preferred Galaxy variant open.

## What visitors may be trying to do

| Intent hypothesis                | First decision                                     | Useful result                                                                                                   | Scenario to test                                                                                                             |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Find a known service             | Does this studio do the work I need?               | A service page with deliverables, example work, limits and an inquiry route                                     | “You need to plan a migration to post-quantum cryptography. | Get timely help                  | How can I reach someone and what should I include? | A short inquiry with an explanation of what happens next                                                        | “A device stops reporting intermittently. You have no technical brief and are unsure what to build first.”                    |

These are recruitment and task hypotheses. They do not describe observed proportions of LEVNAT traffic. Invite potential customers who can relate to each task, with a mix of business owners, operational staff and technical leads.

## Evidence and transfer limits

**Independent research and human contact serve different needs.** Gartner’s March 2026 release reports that 67% of 646 surveyed B2B buyers preferred a rep-free experience. A May release reports that 69% of 645 buyers preferred validating AI-generated insights with a sales representative. Both releases describe August–September 2025 fieldwork and may draw on overlapping research. These are stated preferences among broad B2B buyers. VML’s 2026 research across seven industries and eight markets involved 750 buyers in the report series. Its professional-services sector page emphasizes verifiable technical proof and access to experts, and does not specify its sector subsample. The sector page concerns enterprise purchases by professional-services firms. For a small studio, the practical hypothesis is that credible service detail and an immediate contact route both matter, and a quiz can be one optional way to start. [Gartner, March 9, 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience) · [Gartner, May 20, 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-20-gartner-survey-finds-sixty-nine-percent-of-b-two-b-buyers-turn-to-sales-reps-to-validate-ai-generated-insights) · [VML, The Future Buyer 2026](https://www.vml.com/insight/the-future-buyer-professional-services-report-b2b-2026)

**A guided tool can help when it returns a better decision.** Häubl and Trifts experimentally tested a recommendation agent and comparison matrix in a simulated online store. The recommendation agent reduced search effort and improved the quality of considered alternatives and purchase decisions by the study’s measures. The task was selecting predefined retail products with comparable attributes. A consultancy inquiry may involve an open-ended problem and several plausible services. The study motivates testing whether the Galaxy guidance yields a more appropriate next step, with browsing and contact available independently. [Häubl and Trifts, February 1, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.19.1.4.15178)

**Use simple question presentation and test the words.** A randomized German panel experiment compared a mobile-first survey layout bundle with a desktop-first bundle. For its item-nonresponse model (4,309 respondents), the study found lower nonresponse for desktop/laptop, tablet and paper participants, and no significant difference for smartphone participants. Its online breakoff model (4,016 respondents) found no significant differences across conditions. This study compares bundles in a panel survey, so it cannot isolate the effect of a single answer-list decision in Galaxy. GOV.UK’s question-page guidance recommends starting with one question at a time, supporting Back and Continue, giving valid uncertainty a real response and asking each necessary question once. In 2026 DSIT’s survey team used cognitive testing and pilot data together to identify hard-to-understand technical questions and revise or remove them. For Galaxy, read the question aloud with participants and ask what it means before asking them to choose. [Schwerdtfeger, Weiß and Struminskaya, August 29, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/14707853261483760) · [GOV.UK question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/) · [DSIT questionnaire development, July 16, 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/dsit-public-engagement-survey-20252026/dsit-public-engagement-survey-20252026-technical-report#questionnaire-development)

**Progress gives orientation. Treat retention as a separate outcome.** A 2013 meta-analysis of 32 randomized web-survey experiments found no significant overall reduction in dropoff with a constant progress indicator. Effects varied with the pacing of the indicator. Test whether “Question 2 of 5” helps visitors know where they are and what remains. Show a total only when it is predictable. Completion marks should describe completion and avoid suggesting diagnostic confidence. [Villar, Callegaro and Yang, 2013](https://research.google/pubs/where-am-i-a-meta-analysis-of-experiments-on-the-effects-of-progress-indicators-for-web-surveys/) · [GOV.UK progress guidance](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/#using-progress-indicators)

**3D appeal and task performance can diverge.** In a two-stage eye-tracking and survey study, 31 participants used five campus-map variants. The 2D maps produced shorter fixations and more structured visual search, while the 3D map received higher satisfaction and appeal ratings. A retail study randomly assigned 120 participants to four representations of an IKEA lamp. Adding a recorded AR presentation raised reported engagement, with no significant differences in purchase intent across groups. Maps and lamp representations differ from finding a labeled service answer. Both studies motivate tracking ease of selecting and understanding an answer separately from visual preference. [Sendecki, Skalski and Dzienkowski, September 30, 2026](https://ph.pollub.pl/index.php/iapgos/en/article/view/8277) · [Berg and colleagues, online June 25, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/09593969.2025.2522755)

**The task matters more than just looking at the scene.** Putkonen and colleagues studied 84 participants locating cued targets across 900 real interface images. Interface type, whether a target was present and cue type affected search time more than overall visual complexity. Free viewing and goal-driven search produced different patterns. The study used screen images and did not test animated object selection. For Galaxy, measure a real task such as identifying the correct response, and ask separately what the person thought of the scene. [Putkonen and colleagues, May 2025](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/understanding-visual-search-in-graphical-user-interfaces/)

## What the current Galaxy does

- The explicit 3D quiz trigger and text questionnaire coexist with service links and Contact. On mobile, the first tap on a sphere inspects it and a second tap selects it. Answer labels also exist in a horizontally scrolling HTML strip. At 320 px the browser audit measured 1,120 px of answer controls in a 272 px viewport. The second label could be read in the first view, but only the first button fully fit. The seventh choice remained reachable and selectable. This demonstrates a discoverability risk rather than a measured user failure rate. [Galaxy markup](../../website/src/galaxy/index.html) · [Gesture and control audit](website-design-mapping-audit-2026-11-13.md)
- The five questions each contain seven choices, mapped one per service. The first, second and fifth prompts can invite similar answers about what needs work. Some options combine multiple services or terms, such as “Backend, API or dashboard.” There is no general option for uncertainty or a problem that crosses service areas. Those are content hypotheses to check through paraphrasing and realistic scenarios. [Question and scoring data](../../website/src/galaxy/js/data/quiz-data.js)
- The scoring rules sum answer weights into archetypes and resolve ties using a fixed order. An exhaustive local replay of all 16,807 complete combinations found 3,014 ties for the highest score and no combination that returns the defined `bench` archetype. These are counts in the rules, independent of how often visitors choose a path. A five-answer post-quantum security scenario returns “Project planning & architecture,” whose suggested action is generic. A mixed hardware and reporting case returns hardware debugging. Use these as explicit content validation cases. [Question and scoring data](../../website/src/galaxy/js/data/quiz-data.js) · [Recommendation and summary logic](../../website/src/galaxy/js/project-summary.js)
- The in-page Galaxy Contact form adds answers to the inquiry and assigns its category from the first answer. The result’s working-session link leads to the standalone Contact page with Planning selected. The two paths do not carry the same context. An appropriate result should explain why it fits, give a concrete next step and let the visitor inspect and edit any details they intend to send. [Contact handler and recommendation](../../website/src/galaxy/js/ui.js) · [Result and contact markup](../../website/src/galaxy/index.html)

## Question protocol to test

Before changing the number of questions, record what each answer changes in the result, inquiry or next step. A question that repeats an earlier choice should earn its place through an observable difference in output.

| Decision                                   | Candidate prompt                                     | Candidate answers and effect                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identify the situation                     | “What are you trying to do?”                         | Build something new, improve an existing system, fix an issue, or “I’m not sure where to start.” Use this to choose the next question or offer a discovery route.                                       |
| Identify the outcome                       | “What would a good outcome look like?”               | Examples tailored to the situation: customers can complete a task online, staff stop copying data, a device reports reliably, or we know what to build first. Keep direct paths to specialist services. |
| Identify a constraint only when it matters | “What do you already have that we should work with?” | An existing site, software or device, an example of the problem, a written brief, or “I’m not sure.” Ask follow-ups only if this changes the recommendation or what information is needed.              |
| Agree on the next step                     | “What would help you move forward?”                  | See a relevant service or example, discuss an issue, or plan the first step. Show the reason for the suggested path and any credible second option.                                                     |

Use one plain-language question and a complete vertically reachable option list. Include “I’m not sure” where it represents an honest answer. Give uncertainty a meaningful result, such as a short planning route with examples of what to bring. Keep Previous and Exit explicit. Test select-then-Continue against automatic advance for accidental taps and the effort required to correct them. Separate answers that explain an outcome from details that can wait until the inquiry.

## Interface comparison

| Variant                                   | Interaction                                                                                                                                          | Hypothesis to verify                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A. Current quiz                           | Seven spheres, per-object inspection and a horizontally scrolling answer strip                                                                       | Provides a baseline for how people discover answers, correct mistakes and leave the scene.            |
| B. One HTML answer block                  | One heading and semantic vertical answer group. All choices remain available through normal page scrolling. Previous, Continue and Exit are labeled. | Improves option discovery and correction, with any change in visual appeal measured separately.       |
| C. One HTML answer block with optional 3D | Same block, wording, order, result and state as B. The scene shows matching feedback or can be expanded. A sphere selection updates the same answer. | Preserves visual appeal or helps explain some options while matching the clarity and completion of B. |

Compare A, B and C with the same question wording and recommendation logic first. Comparing versions with different content, result rules and visuals at once makes it hard to tell what caused a change. Test the revised question protocol separately on the clearest interface. If a participant chooses through a sphere, the HTML answer should show the same selection. If they choose through HTML, the scene should reflect that state where visible. Let narrow-screen users complete every step without a horizontal gesture, hover or WebGL. [W3C forms and labels](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/) · [WCAG 2.2 keyboard and pointer gestures](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#input-modalities)

## Test with real visitors

1. **Discovery and terminology.** Run short interviews and cognitive walkthroughs with a small purposive round of about six to eight potential visitors. Ask what they came to do and what they would click first. Have them paraphrase questions and describe differences between ambiguous choices. Use their actual devices where practical. Revisit the wording with a second round after revisions. The sample target is a practical starting point for finding recurring issues. [GOV.UK moderated usability testing](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-moderated-usability-testing)
2. **Scenario tasks.** Start some sessions on Home and others on a deep service page. Keep direct browse and contact tasks unprompted so the test can reveal accidental quiz entry. For the unsure scenario, let visitors decide whether to browse or take the quiz. Use matched scenarios and balance the order of prototypes to reduce learning effects. Include touch on real iPhone and Android devices, and keyboard or screen-reader use with participants who use those inputs.
3. **Recovery tasks.** Ask the participant to change an earlier answer, exit and resume, use a text route when 3D is unavailable, then continue to Contact. Check whether the destination, restored answer, visible focus and inquiry preview match their expectation. Include 320 and 390 CSS px layouts, both themes and reduced motion in engineering QA. Use the [Website design research](../research/website-design-research.md) and [Website mapping research](../research/website-mapping-research.md) for the broader QA matrix.
4. **Recommendation validity.** Before viewing quiz outputs, ask a studio reviewer to define acceptable next steps and evidence to request for each example. Have the participant explain the proposed result and what they would do next in their own words. Ask whether the suggestion fits, why, and what it missed. Compare with the independently defined acceptable outcomes. Include explicit uncertainty, a pure PQC case, and a multi-service case where a second route may be appropriate.
5. **Field validation if traffic supports it.** Define the primary measure, acceptable tradeoff and analysis window in advance. Randomly assign a stable variant for visitors in the test, subject to the site’s privacy and consent approach. Keep task types, channel and device comparisons explicit. Use a pilot baseline and planned detectable effect to determine sample size. With insufficient traffic, report qualitative issues and observed counts with uncertainty. [GOV.UK usability benchmarking](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/measuring-success/usability-benchmarking-a-website-or-whole-service)

### Measures and decisions

| Measure                         | What to record                                                                                                                                                             | Decision it supports                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Direct path and discoverability | Correct first action and unassisted task success by scenario. Note unintended quiz starts and failures to find direct contact.                                             | Keep browse and contact visible. Reject a default that creates recurring wrong turns or blocks a direct path.                                                  |
| Answer interaction              | Ability to find any named option, unintended selections, retries, Previous use, correction success and time to a valid choice.                                             | Advance the HTML block if it reliably resolves hidden-answer and correction issues. Fix any functional access blockers before comparison.                      |
| Recommendation usefulness       | Can the visitor explain the result and identify a suitable next step? Does it match an acceptable outcome for the scenario? Record uncertainty and reasons for a mismatch. | Retain only questions that improve the result, and revise rules that repeatedly produce generic or inappropriate recommendations.                              |
| 3D contribution                 | Whether the scene was used, what it helped the participant understand, perceived appeal and separate task performance.                                                     | Keep 3D prominent if it contributes value without a material cost in task completion, accessibility or performance. Offer optional 3D where evidence is mixed. |
| Inquiry handoff                 | Reached inquiry and completed inquiry are separate outcomes. Inspect whether answers are previewed, editable and carried only with a deliberate action.                    | Prefer the path that preserves visitor intent and avoids duplicate entry or accidental disclosure.                                                             |

For analytics, count the source of entry, step impressions, explicit exits, result views and inquiry starts or confirmed submissions as separate events. Keep a minimal schema such as variant, viewport band, entry type, question ID and elapsed time. Avoid collecting email, inquiry text, raw quiz responses, sensitive project data or full URLs in analytics. Use consented observation for interpreting specific answer choices. Derive completion among starters and inquiry outcomes among eligible sessions, with denominators stated. A session that leaves the quiz for a useful service or Contact can be a success.

Choose a variant after checking the complete journeys on real devices and reviewing outcomes for people seeking direct contact as well as guidance. Preserve both routes if data are inconclusive. Document the open decision and what evidence would change it.
