# Best Traits of Developer Websites

Design reference and creative brief for LEVNAT. Reviewed November 6, 2026.

LEVNAT sells website and application development, integrations and automation, with hardware/software debugging and ongoing support as complementary services. Its website should demonstrate that capability, establish trust and make an inquiry easy. It also needs an experience distinctive enough to remember and voluntarily share.

The strongest direction is not to combine every fashionable effect. It is to choose one recognizable idea, execute it exceptionally well, support it with real work, and make every ordinary interaction feel considered. A beautiful object is presentation. A meaningful response to the visitor's actions is an experience.

The references below are a curated selection, not a verified ranking of the world's most popular sites. Nine are individual practitioners; Lusion is a studio benchmark for creative execution. Observations describe public content and selected visual inspections. Interpretations and LEVNAT proposals are design judgments, not evidence of conversion uplift or guaranteed virality.

## 1. Reference Sites and Their Strongest Lessons

### Bruno Simon

**Model:** creative developer whose portfolio is itself an interactive demonstration.

The portfolio presents a drivable 3D world, with exploration, achievements, a circuit and controls for different input devices. The site also explains its technology and provides development material. Its loaded opening scene has a distinct environment, vehicle, lighting and interaction prompt rather than a conventional content column. [Portfolio and technical background](https://bruno-simon.com/).

**Take:** make the medium demonstrate the skill being sold; build a recognizable world with understandable consequences and discoveries.

**Do not copy:** the vehicle, map, art style or the requirement to play before finding essential business information. A service business needs a fast route to work, services and contact.

### Dennis Snellenberg

**Model:** individual freelance designer/developer with commercial work at the center.

The opening composition gives a real person, name and professional role significant visual presence. Work, About and Contact are direct navigation choices. The Damai project page identifies services, collaborators, location/year, media and a live-site link. [Portfolio](https://dennissnellenberg.com/), [The Damai case](https://dennissnellenberg.com/work/the-damai).

**Take:** confidence of composition, an unmistakable author and project-specific evidence with honest attribution.

**Do not copy:** the portrait, oversized name treatment or exact motion language. Those work as part of his identity, not as a reusable identity for everyone.

### Brittany Chiang

**Model:** frontend-engineering portfolio with professional evidence.

The site separates About, Experience, Projects and Writing. Experience entries identify responsibilities and technologies; projects provide descriptions, images and links. A skip link is present. [Portfolio](https://brittanychiang.com/).

**Take:** make competence inspectable through specific contributions and navigable evidence.

**Do not copy:** another developer's biography, employment history, metrics or resume-first hierarchy. LEVNAT primarily needs to serve buyers of development services, not recruiters.

### Rauno Freiberg

**Model:** interaction designer demonstrating craft through interfaces and prototypes.

The Craft gallery mixes interface explorations, essays, prototypes and production work, with short identifying captions. His interaction-design essay examines responsiveness, interruptibility, familiar metaphors and spatial consistency. [Craft](https://rauno.me/craft), [Invisible Details of Interaction Design](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design).

**Take:** quality is visible in how an interface responds, not only in a static screenshot. Demonstrate a small interaction so precisely that the visitor wants to try it again.

**Do not copy:** obscure navigation or gestures merely because they are interesting. Essential tasks still need recognizable controls and accessible alternatives.

### Emil Kowalski

**Model:** design engineer whose components and explanations demonstrate product quality.

His site links concrete projects including Sonner and Vaul. His animation article distinguishes purposeful feedback and occasional delight from motion that slows repeated tasks. It includes interactive comparisons rather than only assertions. [Personal site](https://emilkowal.ski/), [You Don't Need Animations](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/you-dont-need-animations).

**Take:** assign each animation a job. Use a special moment sparingly; keep frequent controls fast and predictable.

**Do not copy:** a component library's appearance as a complete brand. A polished drawer does not replace positioning, content or a distinctive main experience.

### Paco Coursey

**Model:** design engineer with public software and focused interaction studies.

His homepage connects a concise introduction to projects, writing and contact. Craft examples include dropdowns, filtering, a command palette, video controls and window interactions. [Personal site](https://paco.me/), [Craft](https://paco.me/craft).

**Take:** ordinary interface behavior can be portfolio evidence when it is executed with unusual care. Link claims to things visitors can inspect.

**Do not copy:** adding a command palette to a small website that does not need one. A familiar navigation link is often the better control.

### Josh W. Comeau

**Model:** engineering knowledge demonstrated through interactive explanation.

The homepage organizes articles by topic. His Flexbox guide lets readers manipulate examples while explaining the resulting behavior. The interaction supports understanding rather than sitting beside unrelated promotional copy. [Homepage](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/), [Interactive Flexbox guide](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/interactive-guide-to-flexbox/).

**Take:** let visitors discover why an engineering decision matters by changing something and seeing its consequence.

**Do not copy:** turning a commercial homepage into a long tutorial. Buyers should not need a programming lesson to understand the service.

### Jhey Tompkins

**Model:** designer/engineer with a broad catalog of small, distinctive experiments.

His homepage leads to prototypes and an index with search and ordering controls. The index includes interface, visual and device-oriented explorations, including ESP32-related examples. [Homepage](https://www.jhey.dev/), [Experiment index](https://www.jhey.dev/index).

**Take:** small, focused experiments can create memorable proof of curiosity and technical range. Give experiments names, links and a reason to try them.

**Do not copy:** publishing a large pile of disconnected effects. LEVNAT should curate a few experiments relevant to the problems its customers have.

### Lee Robinson

**Model:** concise engineer/writer website.

The current site presents a short biography, an optional longer version, organized notes and writing. It establishes a direct personal voice without requiring an elaborate introduction. [Personal site](https://leerob.com/).

**Take:** confidence can come from brevity. Remove content that delays the useful information.

**Do not copy:** assuming radical minimalism alone meets this project's expressiveness goal. Clarity is the foundation, not the entire creative direction.

### Lusion

**Model:** creative-development studio, not a solo-developer portfolio.

The current homepage combines an explicit 3D/interactive offer, a large physical-object composition, featured projects and a separate Labs link. The Devin AI case explains the communication challenge and names the services involved. [Studio](https://lusion.co/), [Devin AI case](https://lusion.co/projects/devin_ai/).

**Take:** art direction, materials, framing and storytelling must work together. Present the result of an engineering capability, not just its technology names.

**Do not copy:** studio scale, team claims, client work or an expensive cinematic production pipeline without an appropriate scope. Use this as a craft benchmark, not as LEVNAT's business identity.

## 2. Forty Traits Worth Combining Selectively

These are proposed principles for LEVNAT, synthesized from the references and the business goal. They are not a checklist of features that every reference site implements.

### Identity and Positioning

**01. One recognizable central idea.** The site should be describable without mentioning its accent color: for example, a working product that can be built, connected and repaired. If the description is only "dark site with a floating screen," the idea is not distinctive enough.

**02. A visible author.** Show who is responsible for the work through an approved name, biography, portrait or genuine working material. A domain-derived wordmark is useful, but it cannot carry the entire human trust story. Never invent a team or credentials.

**03. A literal offer.** State website and app development clearly. Explain integrations, debugging and support in ordinary language. Personality and art direction should strengthen the offer, not replace it with an ambiguous slogan.

**04. A specific audience and starting problem.** Give visitors recognizable entry points: a new business website, a first application, disconnected tools, or an existing product that needs investigation. Avoid presenting every technical capability as equally important.

### First Impression and Composition

**05. One signature moment.** A memorable opening needs a deliberately staged event: a system connects, a product changes state, or a fault is revealed and resolved in sample data. A looping rotation is not a narrative by itself.

**06. Immediate visitor agency.** A simple action should produce a clear response. The interesting part must be discoverable without a manual, prolonged onboarding, precision clicking or a compulsory questionnaire.

**07. Readable content before spectacle.** The name, offer and inquiry action must remain available while media loads. Text should not be rendered only inside a canvas or hidden behind an introduction.

**08. A composed first viewport.** Headline, visual subject and primary action need distinct visual ownership. Leave an actual hint of the next section. Recompose the scene on mobile rather than shrinking a desktop arrangement until it becomes a thumbnail.

### Visual Craft

**09. An authored typographic hierarchy.** Choose a small set of text roles with clear contrast in scale, weight and spacing. Large display type belongs to a genuine opening composition, not to every panel and repeated section.

**10. A deliberate layout rhythm.** Alternate a strong visual moment, useful evidence and quiet reading space. Use asymmetric editorial compositions where they help; use familiar alignment where users need to compare information.

**11. A restrained but non-monotonous palette.** Give the main action one consistent color role. Use secondary colors to differentiate meaningful states or categories. Avoid recoloring every surface with variations of one hue or adding unrelated gradients for interest.

**12. Original, inspectable media.** Show actual software, approved project imagery, a real prototype or a relevant custom model. Do not hide the product in dark atmosphere or substitute generic laptop stock images for evidence.

**13. Convincing physical detail in 3D.** Silhouette, proportions, edge treatment, lighting, camera and materials should agree. Good metal needs intentional reflections; good depth needs composition, not indiscriminate glow. An object should still read clearly in a paused frame.

**14. A coherent visual grammar across pages.** Shared controls should feel related, but each page needs a purpose-specific layout. A case study, service page and inquiry form should not all be identical grids with different headings.

### Native Feeling and Motion

**15. Direct manipulation.** When an object can be dragged, the result should track the input. Hover, keyboard and touch need appropriate equivalents; a desktop-only hover trick is not the whole interaction.

**16. Interruptible behavior.** Users should be able to reverse a choice, close an overlay or navigate away without waiting for an animation to finish. Motion should not lock the site into a sequence.

**17. Spatial consistency.** A detail should expand from a meaningful source and return predictably. Changes should communicate relationships rather than make elements appear in unrelated positions. This interpretation follows the interaction principles discussed in [Rauno's essay](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design).

**18. Purposeful motion with frequency limits.** Use motion to explain a change, acknowledge input or deliver a rare surprise. Ordinary navigation and repeated form actions should remain quick. Do not replay an elaborate entrance on every page visit. [Emil's animation discussion](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/you-dont-need-animations).

**19. Complete interaction states.** Hover is only one state. Design focus, press, selected, loading, empty, success, disabled and failure states. A button that looks polished but reports a false success damages the experience.

**20. Familiar controls for familiar tasks.** Use real links, buttons, segmented choices, menus and form inputs. Custom cursors or unusual gestures should be optional embellishments, never prerequisites for navigation or contact.

### Evidence and Content

**21. Work that can be inspected.** Put strong project evidence near the top of the journey. Use an understandable preview, contribution and next action instead of a generic technology-logo wall.

**22. Case studies with a real argument.** Explain the starting problem, constraints, personal contribution, important decisions, delivered scope and evidence. State outcomes only when they are supported. A beautiful screenshot is not a complete case study.

**23. Demonstrations that have substance.** Let a visitor enter sample data, encounter a controlled failure or compare meaningful states. A counter increment or an automatic sequence of green checks is too shallow to carry a flagship experience alone.

**24. Technical depth on demand.** Architecture, implementation notes, repositories and testing details should be reachable for technical readers without overwhelming a non-technical buyer's first screen.

**25. Clear boundaries between evidence types.** Distinguish commissioned work, personal products, independent prototypes and visual experiments. Credit collaborators. Demonstrations are valuable; disguising them as client achievements is not.

### Navigation and Inquiries

**26. A purposeful multi-page structure.** Give major destinations real URLs. Preserve direct loading, browser history and context when moving between a project, relevant service and inquiry.

**27. Progressive disclosure.** Show the decision-making essentials first, then offer deeper detail. Do not ask visitors to parse every dependency, exclusion and technology before they understand what can be built.

**28. Contextual next steps.** An app example should lead to an app discussion; a diagnostic example should lead to an investigation inquiry. Carry the service choice forward without silently replacing an existing project description.

**29. Low-friction contact.** Provide an obvious project inquiry path and a direct email alternative. Ask for useful scope information, not a long qualification survey. Keep paid planning distinct from an ordinary initial inquiry.

**30. Honest and recoverable drafts.** Preserve entered content, expose validation errors, support copy/download where appropriate, and label email drafts accurately. Explain local storage concisely and provide a way to clear it.

### Playfulness and Shareability

**31. Meaningful optional play.** Reward discovery or progress through a working demonstration, not arbitrary points that pretend to measure business health or technical quality. The commercial journey must remain usable without playing.

**32. A short, repeatable interaction loop.** A visitor should be able to make a choice, see a consequence and try a variation. Aim for an understandable first interaction, with additional depth available rather than compulsory.

**33. An artifact worth sharing.** A distinctive system composition, visual experiment or useful diagnostic explanation gives someone a concrete reason to share. A generic "share this website" button does not create that reason.

**34. Restorable, privacy-safe states.** A share link should reopen the interesting demonstration state. Serialize only an explicit allowlist of public sample settings; never include inquiry notes, email addresses, device logs or secrets in a URL or generated image. Sharing must be initiated by the visitor.

### Mobile, Inclusion and Engineering

**35. Mobile-specific art direction.** Keep the visual subject meaningful at phone scale. Reframe the camera, simplify detail and put controls within reach. Do not let the hand or controls obscure the interaction's result.

**36. Inclusive reading and operation.** Preserve semantic HTML, labels, visible focus, keyboard access, readable contrast and usable enlarged text. Touch targets should be comfortably sized; a 44 CSS-pixel design target is a project choice, not a claim about every WCAG AA minimum.

**37. User-controlled spectacle.** Honor reduced-motion preferences, provide a way to pause non-essential continuing motion, and keep audio off until requested. A static or no-WebGL mode should still look intentional and retain the business journey.

**38. Fast core content and progressive enhancement.** Render the useful page before loading optional 3D. Avoid blocking navigation on shader compilation or a large model. Stop unnecessary rendering when offscreen or hidden; test recovery rather than reloading away user state.

**39. Findable and previewable pages.** Use specific titles, useful descriptions, canonical routes and appropriate social preview imagery. A case-study link should represent that case, not send every share to the same generic homepage.

**40. A maintainable body of work.** New case studies, media and service details should be straightforward to add. A visually striking site that cannot be updated will become an outdated performance of expertise rather than evidence of current work.

## 3. What the Current LEVNAT Version Still Lacks

This is a creative assessment of the current implementation, not a new usability study. Passing automated QA establishes a working foundation; it does not establish originality, emotional impact or commercial effectiveness.

| Area          | Current foundation                                     | Creative gap                                                                    |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Structure     | Eight working pages and clear service routes           | The journey is organized, but not yet driven by a memorable central experience  |
| Main visual   | Layered browser/app object with three modes            | Familiar floating-interface imagery; limited visitor influence over the product |
| Interaction   | Mode changes, a queue and a simulated workflow         | Too little depth to demonstrate how a real customer problem gets solved         |
| Evidence      | Clearly labeled independent demonstrations             | No owner-approved client case studies or substantial personal-product stories   |
| Identity      | LEVNAT wordmark and an independent voice               | Not enough distinctive authorship, real working material or visual character    |
| Visual system | Restrained typography, palette and responsive layouts  | Professional baseline, but insufficiently authored composition and imagery      |
| Shareability  | Optional legacy explorers                              | No signature artifact or stateful sharing loop in the new primary experience    |
| Validation    | Automated layout, interaction and accessibility checks | No human comprehension, recall, preference or inquiry study                     |

**Conclusion:** the next step should not be another round of minor color, glow or border changes. It should be a stronger creative concept and better evidence, expressed through one substantially better interaction.

## 4. Recommended Creative Direction for LEVNAT

### A Working Product, Not a Decorative Sculpture

Build one coherent sample product that connects an interface, an API and a device-backed workflow. Let the visitor change the system and understand the result. The demonstration must remain visibly fictional sample data, not a claim about a deployed customer system.

The opening should still lead with websites and applications. Hardware belongs in the supporting story so the business does not look like a computer-repair shop.

| Chapter  | Visitor action                                      | Meaningful consequence                                                                     | Service connection                      |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| Build    | Choose a small product capability                   | The actual sample interface gains a usable flow, not just a different color                | Website and app development             |
| Connect  | Route a sample request between tools                | Inspect validated input, a visible record and a sample notification; explore an error path | APIs and automation                     |
| Debug    | Introduce a controlled fault and inspect its events | Identify the relevant sample signal, apply a bounded change and rerun the scenario         | Software and device-connected debugging |
| Maintain | Choose an agreed care task                          | See a specific test, update or monitoring rule and its scope                               | Ongoing support                         |

These are four chapters of one product, not four unrelated scenes. A visitor may explore one chapter without completing the others. A normal service link and inquiry action remain available throughout.

### The Three Layers of the Experience

**First impression:** a distinctive, inspectable object and a simple action with a satisfying consequence. No compulsory loading ceremony or hidden navigation.

**Evidence:** a short explanation of the engineering decision, relevant genuine work or an explicitly labeled prototype, and optional technical detail.

**Commercial next step:** a service-specific inquiry with the selected interest retained. The visitor should never need to finish a mini-game to contact the developer.

### The Visual Direction to Test

Use a focused engineering atelier identity: tactile materials and precise interaction in the main experience, contrasted with quiet editorial layouts for work and services. Choose a distinctive silhouette, camera and material treatment before choosing decorative effects.

Do not lock the next iteration to the existing lime/charcoal palette merely because it is already implemented. Evaluate visual directions as complete compositions with real content. Keep one direction; do not blend fragments of all ten references.

The main visual must resemble a product someone could actually use. Avoid stacking miniature dashboards whose text is unreadable at normal viewing size. Include a real DOM-based product surface where visitors need to read and operate it; use Three.js for spatial explanation and material presence.

### What Could Be Worth Sharing

A visitor could create a small public sample-system composition, replay a short debugging scenario, or share a useful interactive explanation. A link should restore the selected public state. An optional image could show that state with the LEVNAT identity, without private form fields.

This is a shareability hypothesis, not a promise of viral reach. Evaluate voluntary shares, meaningful visits and inquiries after the experience exists. Do not use fake visitor counts, fabricated leaderboards or forced sharing to unlock information.

## 5. Page Structure That Supports the Experience

| Destination      | Primary job                                                 | Required substance                                                                                |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Home             | Explain the offer and create the signature first impression | Literal service headline, distinctive working demonstration, strong proof preview, direct inquiry |
| Work             | Establish credibility                                       | Curated real work and substantial independent products, with clear labels                         |
| Individual case  | Explain a specific contribution                             | Problem, constraints, role, decisions, inspectable media, supported outcome, relevant next step   |
| Services         | Help a buyer recognize the right engagement                 | Tasks, deliverables, boundaries, indicative pricing and contextual contact                        |
| About / Approach | Establish the person and working relationship               | Approved biography, genuine working material, process and practical expectations                  |
| Lab              | Provide optional depth and repeatable discovery             | A small, curated set of functioning experiments, each with a direct link                          |
| Contact          | Start a useful conversation                                 | Short scope form, direct email, clear submission semantics and recovery                           |

Do not increase the page count just to appear larger. Every added destination must answer a different question. Keep project proof separate from a lab gallery without pretending experiments are commissioned work.

## 6. Choices to Reject

- Copying a famous developer's composition, car, portrait treatment or personality.
- Treating more shaders, particles or glow as a substitute for a strong idea.
- A compulsory game, quiz, intro animation or account before services and contact.
- Generic terminal typing, technology-logo clouds and meaningless skill percentages as the main proof.
- Fake health scores, invented client wins, fabricated testimonials or unsupported business metrics.
- Hardware imagery that makes web/app development disappear from the offer.
- Large, unreadable screen textures where visitors need to inspect actual software.
- Custom cursors that conceal standard interaction behavior or fail on touch.
- Hover-only discovery, scroll hijacking, endless loops and audio that starts without consent.
- Repeating the same rounded-card composition on every page.
- Decorative page transitions that discard scroll position, keyboard focus or entered content.
- A share button with nothing distinctive, useful or personal-safe to share.

## 7. Quality Targets and Their Basis

### Performance

Google's good Core Web Vitals thresholds are LCP at or below 2.5 seconds, INP at or below 200 milliseconds, and CLS at or below 0.1, assessed at the 75th percentile with mobile and desktop segmentation. These are release targets to measure, not results already established for LEVNAT. [Web Vitals guidance](https://web.dev/articles/vitals).

The offer and inquiry action should not depend on the optional scene finishing its download. Define an asset and rendering budget against representative physical devices before committing to the final 3D treatment. A software-rendered screenshot is not evidence of smooth mobile GPU performance.

### Accessibility and Motion

WCAG contrast guidance requires at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for qualifying large text, with stated exceptions. [Contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html).

Automatically starting moving content lasting more than five seconds alongside other content generally needs a pause, stop or hide mechanism unless essential. [Pause, Stop, Hide](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html). Respect the device's preference for reduced non-essential motion. [Reduced-motion media feature](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion).

Use these requirements alongside keyboard, touch, focus, enlarged-text and assistive-technology testing. An automated scan alone does not certify accessibility.

### Creative and Commercial Validation

Use small, explicit research tasks rather than a subjective score presented as science:

- After a brief first look, ask a prospective buyer what the business offers and what they can do next.
- After one interaction, ask what changed and why it matters.
- After a delay, ask which part they remember without prompting.
- Ask them to find relevant work and start an inquiry using a phone.
- Observe whether they voluntarily replay or share the experience; do not equate a prompted click with demand.

An initial five-person study can reveal obvious problems, but is not a reliable population-level conversion estimate. Record the sample, tasks and results rather than claiming that a small preference test proves commercial success.

## 8. Premortem Before the Next Implementation

Assume the next design has already failed. Probability and impact below are planning judgments, not measured statistics.

| Failure mode                                                    | Probability | Impact | Detection                                                                      | Prevention included in the proposed sequence                                              |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The result becomes a collage of famous portfolios               | High        | High   | Review whether the concept can be described without naming its references      | Approve one original concept and reject incompatible visual directions before production  |
| The scene is attractive but still has no meaningful action      | High        | High   | Ask a visitor to explain the consequence of one interaction                    | Prototype one complete input-to-result loop before expanding the scene                    |
| Play hides the service or the contact path                      | High        | High   | First-look and phone inquiry tasks                                             | Keep the offer and ordinary links in HTML; never gate them behind progress                |
| Hardware imagery changes the perceived business                 | Medium      | High   | Ask what service the visitor believes is being sold                            | Lead with a usable website/app surface; introduce devices as supporting context           |
| The scene is slow, unstable or uncomfortable                    | High        | High   | Physical-device profiling, motion preference, lost-context and no-WebGL tests  | Set budgets early, load progressively, support pause and deliberate fallbacks             |
| Attractive demonstrations are mistaken for real client evidence | Medium      | High   | Review every project label, role and outcome against owner-approved material   | Separate commissioned work, personal products and prototypes throughout                   |
| A shared URL or image exposes private information               | Medium      | High   | Inspect serialized state and generated artifacts with adversarial sample input | Allowlist public sample settings; keep inquiry data out; require an explicit share action |
| Navigation or the inquiry loses entered context                 | Medium      | High   | Back/forward, reload, service change and failure journeys                      | Preserve drafts deliberately and keep sending semantics truthful                          |
| The website works only for a precise mouse user                 | High        | High   | Keyboard, touch, enlarged-text and assistive-technology tasks                  | Design equivalent controls and focus behavior with the interaction, not afterward         |

### Proposed Sequence With Safeguards

1. Approve the central idea and collect owner-approved identity and work evidence. Produce a short visual-direction comparison; choose one, not a hybrid of every reference.
2. Prototype a single meaningful interaction with representative content on desktop and phone. Test service comprehension, control visibility and the input-to-result loop before adding effects.
3. Develop the final visual subject, art direction and supporting case-study material. Keep the product readable and the hardware role subordinate.
4. Integrate the experience into the multi-page journey, including service-specific contact and optional privacy-safe sharing.
5. Run device, accessibility, failure-recovery and rendering QA. Inspect actual canvas pixels as well as composed screenshots.
6. Run human comprehension and recall tasks. Consider publication and measurement separately; do not claim virality or conversion improvement in advance.

### Future Acceptance Checklist

These checks belong to the proposed next design. They are not marked complete merely because the current site's automated tests passed.

- [ ] A first-look participant can identify website/app development and find the inquiry path without operating 3D.
- [ ] The chosen concept has a distinctive description and does not rely on a copied visual identity.
- [ ] One complete action has a visible, understandable and reversible consequence.
- [ ] The object and controls remain meaningful on desktop and phone, not merely nonblank.
- [ ] Relevant work has an approved role, evidence and correct project classification.
- [ ] Contact works with keyboard and touch; drafts survive intended navigation and failures.
- [ ] No-WebGL, reduced-motion, pause, hidden-tab and context-recovery states remain useful.
- [ ] Real-device performance and field/lab measurement limits are recorded accurately.
- [ ] Shared URLs and images contain only allowed public sample data.
- [ ] Screen-reader, focus, enlarged-text and contrast checks are complete or explicitly deferred with consequences.
- [ ] Human comprehension and recall results are recorded without pretending to prove population-level conversion.
- [ ] Every high-risk premortem item is verified or explicitly deferred before calling the redesign complete.

## 9. Decision Summary

**Keep:** the working multi-page foundation, honest service scope, ordinary navigation, local draft recovery and explicit demo labels.

**Replace or substantially strengthen:** the generic floating-screen concept, shallow demonstration loops, thin personal identity and lack of substantial project evidence.

**Prioritize:** one original interactive product story, excellent ordinary controls, owner-approved proof, mobile composition and a useful optional sharing artifact.

**Do not implement everything in this reference.** The strongest result will come from selecting a coherent set of traits and executing it deeply. This document is a research deliverable and a proposal for a future iteration; it does not authorize or claim another completed website redesign.
